📖 The Type O Story

Dr. D'Adamo theorizes that Type O is the oldest blood type, dating back to Cro-Magnon hunter-gatherers approximately 40,000 years ago. These early humans thrived on a diet rich in animal protein, with limited grains and no dairy. Type O individuals are said to have a hardy digestive tract, an overactive immune system, and a natural ability to process meat efficiently.

Type O is described as having higher levels of stomach acid, making them well-suited to digesting meat. However, this same trait may increase susceptibility to ulcers. Dr. D'Adamo recommends vigorous aerobic exercise such as jogging, cycling, or martial arts to help manage the Type O stress response.

📖 The Type A Story

Dr. D'Adamo places the emergence of Type A around 20,000 years ago, coinciding with the agricultural revolution. As humans transitioned from hunting to farming, Type A allegedly evolved to thrive on a plant-based diet rich in grains, vegetables, and legumes. Type A individuals are described as having lower stomach acid and a sensitive immune system, making them better suited to vegetarian proteins than heavy meats.

Type A is advised to avoid red meat entirely, focusing instead on soy proteins, fish, and poultry in moderation. Stress management is critical for Type A — Dr. D'Adamo recommends calming exercises like yoga, tai chi, and meditation, as Type A is said to produce higher cortisol levels under stress.

📖 The Type B Story

Dr. D'Adamo theorizes that Type B emerged approximately 10,000–15,000 years ago among nomadic pastoral tribes of the Himalayan highlands and Mongolian steppes. These populations herded animals and consumed dairy, meat, and cultivated grains. Type B is described as the most balanced blood type — able to tolerate a wider variety of foods than any other type.

Type B individuals are said to have a strong immune system, a flexible digestive system, and the best ability to adapt to dairy products. However, Dr. D'Adamo warns that chicken contains a lectin that can trigger immune reactions in Type B, potentially leading to strokes and autoimmune disorders. The biggest weight-gain triggers for Type B are said to be corn, wheat, buckwheat, lentils, tomatoes, peanuts, and sesame seeds.

📖 The Type AB Story

Type AB is described as the youngest and rarest blood type, emerging less than 1,000 years ago. Dr. D'Adamo characterizes it as a genetic fusion of Types A and B — carrying both antigens but inheriting Type A's low stomach acid and Type B's tolerance for dairy. This combination makes Type AB uniquely suited to a mixed diet with an emphasis on seafood, tofu, cultured dairy, and green vegetables.

Because of low stomach acid, Type AB is advised to avoid large portions of red meat, caffeine, alcohol, and all smoked or cured meats. Dr. D'Adamo recommends smaller, more frequent meals and avoiding combining starches and proteins in the same meal for optimal digestion. Exercise should blend calming activities (yoga, tai chi) with moderate aerobic exercise (hiking, swimming, cycling).
